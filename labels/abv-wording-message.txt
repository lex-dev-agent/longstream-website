I looked into the ABV wording before we lock the label.

"38% ABV" is fine. MPI's own labelling guide says the alcohol amount has to be shown as "% Alc/Vol or mL/100 mL ... OR words that mean the same thing" — so the exact abbreviation isn't fixed, and % ABV means the same thing.

Food Standards Australia New Zealand write the actual rule (Standard 2.7.1), and on their own site they use "5% ABV" as the example of how to show it.

There's also nothing requiring a decimal, so 38% and 38.0% are both legal. If anything 38% is the safer one — the stated strength has to be accurate within a tolerance, and 38.0% claims tighter precision than 38% does, so it gives us less room if a batch comes out slightly off.

Totally happy to go with 38.0% Alc/Vol if you prefer the look of it. Just wanted to check we weren't being forced into it. One thing either way — we should use the same wording on both labels.

FSANZ: https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/labelling/Labelling-of-alcoholic-beverages
